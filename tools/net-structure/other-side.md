Perfect. Up until now we've mostly been building the **Connectivity Layer**:

```text
Fiber
Subsea
Carriers
Transit
IXPs
Mobile Networks
Satellite Networks
```

But the other half of the Internet is the **Service Layer**:

```text
Cloud
CDN
DNS
Security
Developer Platforms
Identity
Source Control
AI
Content Platforms
```

This is where AWS, Cloudflare, GitHub, Google, Vercel, etc. live.

For your giant map I'd separate them by function.

# THE "OTHER SIDE OF THE INTERNET"

If you collapse everything we've covered, the non-connectivity side of your giant map roughly becomes:

```text
Internet Services Layer

├── Hyperscalers
│   ├── AWS
│   ├── Azure
│   ├── Google Cloud
│   ├── Oracle
│   ├── Alibaba
│   └── Tencent
│
├── CDN & Edge
│   ├── Cloudflare
│   ├── Akamai
│   ├── Fastly
│   └── Edgio
│
├── DNS
│   ├── Verisign
│   ├── Cloudflare DNS
│   ├── Route53
│   ├── Google DNS
│   ├── Quad9
│   └── NS1
│
├── Identity & Security
│   ├── DigiCert
│   ├── Let's Encrypt
│   └── Sectigo

├── Developer Platforms
│   ├── GitHub

│   └── Fly.io

├── AI Infrastructure

    ├── Google

    ├── TikTok
    └── Spotify
```

When you combine this with the carrier/backbone/subsea side you've already mapped, you start getting a genuinely useful "Internet Dependency Graph" showing not just who provides access to the Internet, but who provides the services that make the modern Internet function.
